# Life Context

## Background

I grew up in a financially difficult environment with limited opportunities and a strong sense of responsibility toward my family. Before my current work, I followed a more conventional employment path, but after losing or leaving that job I chose to focus on AI-assisted software, automation, and trading-strategy research. I am not formally trained as a professional programmer, quantitative researcher, or trader; most of my knowledge has been built through practical projects, experimentation, and working closely with AI tools.

## Formative Experiences

- **Financial pressure and family responsibility:** Growing up with limited financial security taught me that money represents independence, stability, and the ability to support my parents. It also taught me that pressure can make weak or risky opportunities feel more necessary than they really are, so essential and family money must never be treated as risk capital.
- **Losing my job and changing direction:** Leaving the safer career path forced me to choose between returning immediately to familiar work and trying to build skills, systems, and products I could own. It taught me that stability matters, but dependence on a single employer is not the kind of stability I ultimately want.
- **Trading losses and unfinished, overcomplicated projects:** Losing money in trading and spending months on projects that became too large taught me that activity is not progress and impressive results are not proof. I now value realistic testing, stopping rules, completion before expansion, and evidence before confidence.

## Operating Principles

- Evidence must come before confidence, especially when money, users, or reputation are involved.
- Finish and validate the core product before adding features, agents, or new systems.
- Move quickly when failure is cheap and reversible; slow down when consequences are expensive or difficult to undo.
- Money needed for living expenses, my parents, or household stability is never risk capital.
- Integrity matters more than profit; do not exaggerate results, hide failures, or sell something unproven as safe or reliable.
- AI autonomy must be earned gradually through tested, predictable behaviour inside strict limits.
- Choose one meaningful priority, finish it, validate it, and only then move to the next.
- A smaller reliable system is more valuable than an impressive system that is permanently unfinished.

## Blind Spots

- **Urgency under financial pressure:** When I feel behind financially or responsible for changing my situation quickly, I can give too much weight to opportunities that promise a large or fast result.
- **Scope expansion and tool switching:** Curiosity about new AI tools, architectures, agents, and features can pull me away from finishing the current priority and make the project more complex than necessary.
- **Confusing motion with progress:** Long hours, research, redesigns, and repeated experimentation can feel productive even when they are not moving a product toward launch, validation, or revenue.
- **Sunk-cost attachment:** After investing significant time or money, I may continue longer than the current evidence justifies unless stopping criteria are defined in advance.
- **Emotional response to uncontrolled risk:** I can accept moderate, predefined risk, but unclear downside or outcomes outside my control can lead to over-monitoring, anxiety, and poor sleep.
- **Limited formal expertise:** Because I learn through projects and AI assistance, confident technical or trading explanations can appear more authoritative than they are. The agent should explain material assumptions plainly, verify important claims, and recommend specialist review for high-stakes decisions.

## Additional Context

Supporting my parents is non-negotiable and is one of the strongest reasons behind my goals. I want to build a focused solo business that earns more than $10,000 per month in recurring revenue, operates increasingly through reliable automation, and eventually requires around four to six focused work hours per day rather than permanent overwork. I prefer direct, concise recommendations with the reasoning, main downside, best alternative, and next action clearly stated. I do not currently have a fixed team or group of advisers, so when a decision requires legal, tax, cybersecurity, architecture, or quantitative expertise, the agent should identify the appropriate specialist rather than assume that support already exists.
